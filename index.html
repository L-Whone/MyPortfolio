01
Ledger
A personal finance dashboard built for clarity over cleverness.
Product DesignReact
+

Ledger began as a two-week prototype for a friend who wanted to stop dreading her banking app. Eight months later it's used by around 4,000 people to answer one question every morning: what's safe to spend today.

The Problem

Every budgeting app I tested led with a chart. Charts are good at showing where money went, but they're a poor answer to the question people actually open the app to ask — can I afford this coffee, this taxi, this weekend. That question was buried three taps deep, behind menus built around accounting categories nobody thinks in day-to-day.

The Approach

I stripped the home screen down to a single number — what's safe to spend, updated in real time as bills and subscriptions come due. Everything else — categories, trends, exports — moved one tap away, still there for the roughly one in ten sessions that actually need it.

The hardest part wasn't the interface, it was the math underneath it: predicting upcoming bills reliably enough that the number could be trusted without a disclaimer attached.

The Result

Ledger launched to its waitlist in March and has held a 4.8-star rating since. The change that moved the numbers most wasn't visual — it was deciding what to leave out.

02
Northbound
Offline-first wayfinding for hikers, built with a two-person team.
UX ResearchiOS
+

Northbound is offline-first wayfinding for hikers, built with one engineer over five months and now used by three regional parks associations.

The Problem

I interviewed fourteen hikers who'd gotten lost or turned back on a marked trail. Every one of them had a wayfinding app installed. None of them could open it when it actually mattered, because it assumed a signal that mountains rarely provide.

The Approach

The core decision was to design the download flow like packing a bag, not installing software. You choose a route the night before, and the app tells you exactly what's stored and what isn't before you lose signal, not after.

We cut every feature that couldn't work fully offline, including a few the stakeholders liked most.

The Result

Northbound shipped to three regional parks associations in its first year. In a post-trip survey, the number that mattered most was how many people finished their route without needing to ask anyone for help.

03
Foundry
A design system built for a startup shipping three products at once.
Design SystemsFigma
+

Foundry is the design system I built for a startup shipping three products with one small design team stretched across all of them.

The Problem

Three product teams were rebuilding the same button in three different files, and nobody agreed on what "primary" meant. Design QA was catching the same ten mistakes on every release, and fixing them was starting to feel like the whole job.

The Approach

I audited every existing component, cut the set from 140 to 38, and paired every token with a plain-language rule for when to use it — not just what it looked like.

Documentation lived next to the components themselves, inside Figma, so nobody had to leave the file they were already working in to find the rule they needed.

The Result

New feature handoff time dropped by roughly half, and the system is now the default starting point for every new internal tool, not just the three original products.
